# How to play MC Wiki Race

This page spells out everything, including every advancement, the hidden ones too, and the game's secrets. If you'd rather find those yourself, play first and come back later.

## The goal

You get a start article and a target article from [minecraft.wiki](https://minecraft.wiki), and you get from one to the other using only the links inside the articles. Every click counts, and with the Timer on so does the clock. The articles are the live wiki's, fetched as you play.

## Your first game

Open [mcwikirace.com](https://mcwikirace.com/): a start and a target are already rolled on the cards. Press **Start game**, then click links in each article until you land on the target, and the result window shows how you did. The first game is a [Race](#the-four-games) on [Everyday](#articles) articles at [Normal](#difficulty) difficulty, a good way to learn.

## The setup screen

- **Daily challenge**: today's game, the same for everyone (see [The daily](#the-daily)).
- **Your game**: one line that sums up the game, the extra rules, the articles and the difficulty.
- The cards: **Start** and **Target**, plus **Checkpoint** cards in a Relay, or a **Find one of each** card in a Scavenger hunt of kinds. Each shows its article's picture and description.
- **🎲 Reroll** on a card rolls a new article for it. Or type almost any article on the wiki into the box under it ("or type an article…") and press Enter (version pages, the big lists of game data and pages that aren't articles, such as tutorials, are left out).
- **Start game**, with par beside it: the fewest clicks the game can be done in.
- **🎲 Reroll all** rolls every card, and **⇄ Swap** swaps the start and the target: a Relay's checkpoints go in reverse, and a Round trip goes round the same loop from the target (it's hidden under Blind target and in a hunt of kinds). **🎲 Random game** sets every setting below the cards at random, then rolls new cards; the settings beside the title stay as they are.
- **Copy challenge link** copies a link that opens this exact game (see [Challenge links](#challenge-links)).

Under those come three steps: **1 · Choose a game**, **2 · Extra rules** and **3 · Articles and difficulty**.

## The four games

Pick one of the tiles; Race is the default. The strip under them explains the game and holds its one option.

| Game | Tile | What you do | Option |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Race** | A → B | Get from the start to the target in as few clicks as you can. | |
| **Relay** | A → 1 → 2 → B | Visit each checkpoint, in order, before the target counts. A later checkpoint reached early is just an ordinary page. | **Checkpoints**: 1, 2 or 3 (1 by default) |
| **Scavenger hunt** | A → ✓ ✓ ✓ | Instead of one target, find 3 things in any order: one of each of 3 kinds, or 3 articles. | **Find**: 3 kinds or 3 articles (kinds by default) |
| **Exact clicks** | A → → → → B | Reach the target in exactly par + the clicks you pick, never on a page twice. | **Clicks**: par + 1 to par + 5, or Random (par + 2 by default) |

- A kind is a wiki category, such as Hostile mobs, shown with a few of its best-known members as examples. Rolled kinds come from different topics and share no page, so a page usually finds just one (kinds you write into a link yourself may share), and the start never counts.
- In Exact clicks there's no Back, every page you've visited goes dead, and landing on the target too soon loses, as does running out of clicks. There's always a route of the count: the result shows one.

## Extra rules

These are checkboxes that go with any game, and most combine.

| Rule | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Round trip** | Then go back to the start, never through a page of the way there. |
| **No Back** | The Back button is gone: every click is final. |
| **Blind target** | The target is hidden until you start, then you only get its name. A Relay shows each stop once you reach the one before. |
| **Top links only** | Only the links above an article's first heading work: its opening text, infobox and the notes at the top. Routes get much longer. |
| **Six clicks** | Finish in 6 clicks or fewer, or lose: checkpoints, finds and the way home included. Back counts. |
| **Distance** | Always shows how many clicks your next stop is from your page, by the shortest route: the target, a checkpoint, the nearest find or the start on the way back. A click that gets you closer is cheered. |

A few don't combine, and their box greys out with the reason. Six clicks is off with Exact clicks ("Off: Exact clicks has its own count.") and with Top links only ("Off: with Top links only, routes are often longer than six clicks."). Round trip is off with Exact clicks ("Off: Exact clicks never visits a page twice.").

- **Round trip**: in a Relay some checkpoints come after the target, on the way back. Once you reach the target, the pages of the way there go dead and Back starts again from there. If there's no way back that doesn't retrace your steps, the page is marked ⛔ and only giving up is left.
- **Blind target**: the hidden cards say Hidden and when they'll show. Par waits until the game starts, and the ? peek button is gone.
- **Top links only**: the rest of each article folds away under a bar; **Show it** opens it to read, but its links stay dead.
- **Distance**: the header shows "3 clicks to go", "No route from here", or "Distance unknown" on a page the wiki's offline copy doesn't have, and a click that gets closer dings and sparkles. A click that doesn't gets nothing.

## Articles

The **Articles** row picks the pool, the articles the cards are drawn from.

| Pool | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Popular** | The best-known game content, the 30% of it most linked across the wiki. A good place to start. |
| **Everyday** | Every block, item, mob, biome, structure, enchantment and effect (the Content row adds more). Each topic is equally likely, and the start and target always come from different ones. |
| **Deep cuts** | The least-linked third of each topic, so lesser-known pages. Think Camel Husk or Fatal Poison rather than Creeper. |
| **Chaos** | Any article on the wiki, history subpages, most lists and April Fools jokes included. Expect obscure starting points. |

Everyday is the default. Popular and Deep cuts pick topics the same way, while Chaos draws from every article alike and its cards may share a topic. Every rolled card has a route to the others, going by the wiki's offline copy (a download of the whole wiki, dated in the footer), though an article you type in isn't checked.

## Difficulty

The **Difficulty** row decides which links work. Normal is the default.

| Difficulty | What changes |
|---|---|
| **Relaxed** | The link tables at the bottom of articles (navboxes) are there, so almost any sensible click gets you closer. |
| **Normal** | The link tables at the bottom of articles (navboxes) are hidden, so you find your way through the article itself. |
| **Hard** | No navboxes, and links to big overview pages (hubs: Block, Item, Mob, Crafting, Java Edition…) are dead too. |

## Target topic and par

The **Target** row has two choices, both Any by default. The topic (**Any topic**, **Blocks**, **Items**, **Mobs**, **Biomes & structures**, **Enchantments & effects**) takes targets only from that topic. Outside Chaos the start always comes from another topic. The par (**Any par**, **Par 1** to **Par 4**, **Par 5+**) picks how many clicks the target is from the start, at the fewest.

Most pairs are par 2, and par 3 or more is rare in Relaxed. When no start has a target at the par you picked, the roll takes the nearest par that has one and says so, and a par no start can have is greyed out. Scavenger has no target, so it hides the par choice.

## Content and spin-offs

The pool leaves some pages out. The **Content** row adds them back:

| Content | What it adds |
|---|---|
| **April Fools** | Joke blocks, items and mobs from April Fools updates. |
| **Removed & unused** | Things taken out of the game, or in its files but never used. |
| **Education** | Blocks, items and mobs from Minecraft Education. |
| **Books & comics** | Objects from the novels and comics. |
| **Variants** | Stairs, slabs, walls, dyes, pottery sherds, banner patterns, music discs and smithing templates. |
| **Mechanics** | How the game works: breeding, brewing, game rules, achievements and more. |

In Popular, Everyday and Deep cuts, each Content box or spin-off you tick is one more group, as likely to be drawn as a topic. In Chaos every page is equally likely.

The **Spin-offs** row does the same for Dungeons, Dungeons II, Legends, Earth, Story Mode and Movie, with **All** to tick every one. A spin-off's pages can be rolled, and links to them work, only while it's on. In Chaos the Content boxes are ticked and greyed out, since every article is in already.

## During a game

The header shows the game and its rules as labels, with how far along you are ("1 of 2 checkpoints reached", "Found 1 of 3", "On the way there", "On the way back"), and under Exact clicks a reminder to land on the target with your last click, not before. Under them is your route: stops done (✓), the one you're heading for, and those ahead (dashed). The **?** button shows the description of what you're looking for, and reaching a checkpoint or a find shows a banner naming what comes next.

On the right are your **Clicks** ("of 6" under Six clicks, "of exactly 7" under Exact clicks, with a dot a click), **Par**, **Time**, the items and note block buttons, sound, **← Back** and **Give up**. **Your path** runs down the side; **Hide** folds it away. On a phone held upright it becomes one line of breadcrumbs.

## Links in the article

- Live links light up on hover. Links to a page already on your path are purple and dotted, and say "Already on your path".
- Dead links are dimmed and do nothing but a villager's "hrmm": files, categories, tutorials and other non-article pages, red links, other links to pages that don't exist ("Off limits: this page doesn't exist"), links off the wiki, version pages, and the few big list pages of game data that link to nearly everything ("Off limits: a version or list page"). The rules' dead links say why too: Hard's overview pages ("Off limits on Hard: a big overview page"), the lists of what an advancement asks for on Normal and Hard, since they count as navboxes ("Off limits on Normal and Hard: this list counts as a navbox"), everything below the top under Top links only, the pages you've visited under Exact clicks, and your way there once a Round trip turns back.
- A redirect to the target counts as reaching it.
- A page with no live links says so in a banner: go Back, or give up.
- The wiki's own features work: crafting grids cycle through their recipes, tabs switch, folding boxes fold, and the speakers in a Sounds table play.

## Back, the clock and giving up

Back returns to the previous article and counts as a click. The clock pauses while an article loads, and while the Give up dialog is open, but keeps running while you peek. If an article fails to load you get **Retry** and **Cancel**, with the clock stopped, and whether the wiki couldn't be reached or sent back an error.

**Give up** asks first ("Give up?"), with **Keep going**, the main button, to change your mind. While a game is on, closing the tab, reloading or the browser's Back asks first with the browser's own dialog (except on iOS Safari, which never shows it).

## The result

The result window opens with "🎉 You made it!" (or "🎉 You found them all!"), or "Out of clicks", "Too soon" or "You gave up". Under it are your game's labels, then tiles for **Clicks**, **Par**, **Time** and **Per click** (just the first two with the Timer off), your personal best, the XP the game earned, your path and the shortest route.

Beside each page of your path is the fewest clicks from it to what was next; in red, a click that got no closer. For Exact clicks the route column shows one way in exactly your count instead. The buttons are **Share**, **Copy challenge link**, **Same cards again** (the same game from the start) and **New game** (back to the setup screen with new cards).

## Par and golf scores

Par is the fewest clicks a game can be done in, going by the wiki's offline copy ([how that's made](wiki-data.md)), shown beside **Start game** and in the header from the start (under Blind target, once the game starts). A win is named as in golf:

| Score | Means |
|---|---|
| Hole in one! | par 1 in one click |
| Par | even with par |
| Bogey, Double bogey, Triple bogey | 1, 2 or 3 over par |
| Birdie, Eagle, Albatross | 1, 2 or 3 under par |
| N over par, N under par | anything further |

You can only beat par where the live wiki has a link the offline copy doesn't. Exact clicks gets no golf score, since every win is par plus the count you picked. The result names the offline copy par went by, or says a page you played isn't in it, so par is unknown.

## The missed shortcut

Under your path, **Show a shortcut you missed** opens the shortcut from your costliest detour: a page you were on, and the click from it that would have saved you two or more. For example: "From Dirt, Bone Meal was 1 click from Crafting: 3 clicks quicker." It never points at the start or a checkpoint (the shortest route already shows those), and Exact clicks and Share leave it out.

## Share and personal bests

**Share** copies your game (a daily's with its day and year), the route, your result with par and its golf name, your path with each page's count in brackets, and a link to play it. Under Blind target it names only the start and leaves your path out, so it gives nothing away.

Your personal best for a game is the fewest clicks, then the fastest time, among games with the same cards and rules (an Exact clicks count is a game of its own). Beat it and you get "New personal best for this game!", a fanfare and gold fireworks. A game with the Timer off is compared on clicks alone.

## Recent games

**Recent games** lists your last 15 games (before your first, it says so): the game, the route, the result, the time and when. A win's badge is green, a loss's orange and giving up grey, and **Best** marks each game's personal best, out of the last 50 games, which your browser keeps. **Clear history** deletes them all, personal bests, dailies and the daily streak included, after asking; your advancements and XP stay.

## The daily

**📅 Play the daily** loads today's challenge: the same for everyone on the same date, on Normal plus one rule. That rule is one of No Back, Blind target, a Relay of 1 or 2 checkpoints, Hard (No navboxes and No hubs), Six clicks, Round trip, Exact clicks (par + 2, 3 or 4) or a Scavenger hunt.
Every daily can be done, going by the offline copy, and a Six clicks daily has par 4 or less, leaving two clicks to spare.

Any change to the cards or rules makes it an ordinary game, and the game tells you so. A daily's Share and challenge link open that day's daily.

## Streaks and past dailies

The line under the daily shows your streak, your best, and the days won of those played ("🔥 3-day streak · Best 7 · Won 12 of 14 days"). The streak counts days in a row won on their own day, and holds until today's is due ("play today to keep it").

**Past dailies** opens a calendar of every daily so far, each day marked **Won on the day**, **Won later**, **Not won** or **Not played**. You can catch up on a past day, but it doesn't mend a streak, and a day that hasn't come yet can't be played. The first win of a daily gets extra fireworks, a creeper among them.

## Challenge links

**Copy challenge link**, on the setup screen or in the result, copies a link that opens this exact game: its cards, game, rules, difficulty and count. If your browser won't let the page copy it, the link (or in the result, Share's text too) shows under the buttons to copy by hand.
It looks like `https://mcwikirace.com/#g=…`, sealed so the cards aren't readable at a glance. The target topic, target par and Content aren't in it: whoever opens it plays with their own Content boxes, though a card's own spin-off always counts.
Under Blind target it picks the hidden cards first, and they stay hidden for whoever opens the link. A link cut short rolls new cards instead, and says it's damaged.

### Writing one by hand

You can also write a link's keys after the `#` yourself, joined with `&`, for example `https://mcwikirace.com/#start=Dirt&target=Iron%20Ingot&rules=nonav` (a space in a title is `%20`).

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| `start=` and `target=` | The pair, by article title. |
| `via=` | A checkpoint, in order, up to three. With `scavenger` in `rules`, the target and these are the 3 articles to find. |
| `kind=` | One of Scavenger's kinds instead of a target, by its category's name (`kind=Hostile%20mobs`), up to three. |
| `rules=` | Rule keys, comma-separated: `noback`, `blind`, `lead` (Top links only), `distance`, `checkpoint` (Relay), `roundtrip`, `scavenger`, `limit` (Six clicks), `exact`, and `nonav` for Normal or `nonav,nohubs` for Hard. |
| `back=` | With Round trip, how many checkpoints come after the target. |
| `extra=` | With Exact clicks, the clicks over par, 1 to 5. |
| `daily` or `daily=YYYY-MM-DD` | Today's daily, or a past day's. |

A link without `rules` plays on Relaxed. `#rules=…` on its own sets the rules for the cards rolled. An article that doesn't exist, or that the game leaves out, rolls new cards instead.

## Settings

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Sound** | Minecraft's own sounds, fetched from the wiki. The speaker in the game header mutes them too. |
| **Timer** | A clock while you play, and your time in the result. Off: no clock, and no time is recorded. |
| **Effects** | Sparks into the click count, fireworks for a win, XP orbs. The items' own explosions and fireworks stay either way. |
| **Reduce motion** | Stops everything that moves by itself: sparks, the background, moving pictures (shown as their first frame), and the crafting grids, which get a ↻ button instead. Sounds still play. |
| **Theme** | **Auto** follows your system, or **Light** or **Dark**. The article is drawn in the wiki's matching theme. |

These sit beside the title. By default **Sound**, **Timer** and **Effects** are on, **Reduce motion** is off and **Theme** is **Auto**. When your system asks for reduced motion, **Reduce motion** is ticked and held. While **Reduce motion** is on, either way, **Effects** shows unticked and can't be changed.

## The background

Behind the setup screen is a picture of the wiki itself, picked in the **Background** box beside **Theme**. **Show** picks **Auto: constellation, redstone map on phones**, **Constellation**, **Redstone map** or **Off**.

- The constellation is each topic's best-linked articles as stars, joined to those they link with, with pulses playing games of their own along the links. Stars near your mouse make way and the nearest shows its name.
- The redstone map is every article but subpages and spin-off pages as one block, grouped like a world map's biomes, brighter the more it's linked. Your mouse is a torch.
- **Wave from the start** lights every page by how many clicks it is from the start. **Show the route (spoiler)** draws the shortest route between your cards. **Roll by itself** marks random pairs of its own, in grey, until you change anything on the setup screen, then marks your cards in their own colours. By default **Show** is **Auto**, with **Roll by itself** on and the other two off.

The ✦ button in the bottom-right corner (beside the title on a phone) hides everything but the background; ✕ or Escape brings it back. Above the footer, a line of facts about the wiki and your cards changes every 7 seconds.

## The note block

The note block button, in the bottom-right corner on the setup screen (beside the title on a phone) and in the game header, picks what your clicks play. Each click climbs a scale, Back falls two notes (a mob head plays its sound once), and a win plays a fanfare on the instrument underneath. In its window, **Underneath** picks the block it stands on, and so the instrument:

| Block | Instrument | Block | Instrument | Block | Instrument |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dirt | Harp | Block of Gold | Bell | Soul Sand | Cow bell |
| Oak Planks | Bass | Clay | Flute | Pumpkin | Didgeridoo |
| Stone | Bass drum | Packed Ice | Chime | Block of Emerald | Bit |
| Sand | Snare drum | White Wool | Guitar | Hay Bale | Banjo |
| Glass | Hat | Bone Block | Xylophone | Glowstone | Pling |
| Block of Iron | Iron xylophone | Block of Copper | Trumpet | Exposed, Weathered, Oxidized Copper | Exposed, Weathered, Oxidized trumpet |

**On top** puts a mob head there instead, which plays its mob: the Skeleton Skull, Wither Skeleton Skull, Zombie Head, Creeper Head, Piglin Head or Dragon Head. Click the note block itself to hear it. A click's sparks take its note's colour.

## Items

The TNT button, next to the note block's, opens a hotbar of seven items that blow up the page itself. Pick one, then click anywhere to use it; a right click or Escape puts it away (with a finger, the ✕ on the line at the top).

| Item | What clicks do |
|---|---|
| **TNT** | Click to place TNT, and click a placed one to light it. It goes off 4 seconds later. |
| **End Crystal** | Click to place a crystal, and hit one to set it off: the biggest blast. |
| **Ghast Fireball** | Click where the ghast should aim. Hit a fireball in flight to send it back. |
| **Creeper** | Click to let a creeper loose. It follows your pointer; hit it to knock it back. Let it get close and it hisses and blows. |
| **Bed** | Click to place a bed, and click one to sleep in it: "You can't sleep here", and it blows up. |
| **Respawn Anchor** | Click to place an anchor, click one to charge it, and once more to set your spawn "in the Overworld", when it blows up. |
| **Firework Rocket** | Click to launch a firework. It breaks nothing. |

A blast throws the pieces of the page around it, the article's included, and sets off what's near: TNT is lit, crystals go off, beds break, creepers and ghasts die or are knocked back, and an anchor stands, being blast-proof.
While the page is in pieces nothing on it takes clicks. A bar at the bottom offers **🧱 Rebuild**, which puts it all back, and in a game **Give up**, which ends it without asking. Items never count against you, but the clock keeps running.

## The TNT easter egg

On the TNT article, a blast that reaches the infobox's big picture of TNT lights it, as a blast lights TNT in Minecraft. It hisses, flashes, and then goes off bigger than any item, earning Light Reading.

## XP

XP works as Minecraft's, toward levels on Minecraft's own curve, and is never taken away. The bar, with your level over it, shows in the result, where the game's XP flies in as orbs and the bar says how much more the next level takes, and beside the Advancements button.

- A win: 2 × par, 3 more at par and 6 under par (not in Exact clicks), and 2 more for each of Round trip, No Back, Blind target, Top links only, Six clicks and Hard. The first win of the day's daily is 10 more.
- Any game, won or not: 1 for each article you've never seen before.
- An advancement: 5 for a task, 10 for a goal, 25 for a challenge.

A loss or giving up earns nothing of its own, nor do the items, Rebuild or Distance, and clicks themselves earn nothing, so rushing never pays; only pages new to you add a little.

## What's saved in your browser

There are no accounts: everything is kept in this browser's storage, so another browser or device starts fresh. **Clear history** empties your last 50 games, personal bests and dailies; **Reset advancements** empties the advancements, your XP and the counts toward them. Your settings, the note block's pick, the background's settings and whether your path is hidden stay until you change them.
