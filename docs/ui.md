# Themes, pictures, sounds, the background, the game header and phones

Part of the architecture, which is all in `index.html`.

## Themes

A theme is the set of CSS variables on `:root` (colours, `--font`, `--radius*`) plus `color-scheme`, which also decides the article's wiki theme (`themeIsDark()` reads the computed `color-scheme`, falling back to `prefers-color-scheme` when it is ambiguous). Built in: Auto (no `data-theme` attribute, follows the OS), Light and Dark (`data-theme="light"`/`"dark"`). Auto's dark rule is `:root:not([data-theme])`, so no other theme ever picks up the dark variables. A page serving the game can add themes without editing this file: a `<link rel="stylesheet">` or `<style>` in `<head>` with `data-mwr-theme="Name"` that defines `:root[data-theme="Name"]`, plus `data-mwr-default` to make it the default. The names `auto`, `light` and `dark` belong to the built-ins (a host theme named `auto` could never apply). The `#theme-boot` script at the top of `<body>` runs before first paint: it builds `THEMES` (used by the main script) from those elements and applies the saved theme if it still exists, else the default, else Auto. It is not `<script>` followed by a newline on purpose, so the syntax-check regex still finds the main script. The picker on the setup screen calls `setTheme()`. The background behind the setup screen takes its colours from the theme too: `--viz-<topic>` for each topic (`--viz-blocks`, `--viz-items`, `--viz-mobs`, `--viz-world`, `--viz-magic`), `--viz-redstone` for the wave and `--viz-opacity` for how strong it all is; a host theme that leaves them out gets the light ones.

## Pictures

Thumbnails are requested at 400 px (`pithumbsize`). Every setup card and the path sidebar also show a large faded copy (`.art`, at `--art-opacity`, which themes can set) behind their content and cut off by the box's edges: the card's article, and in the sidebar `objective()`. The sidebar's is hidden on narrow screens. Pictures are drawn with hard edges (`image-rendering: pixelated`), except ones whose URL has `/thumb/`: the wiki serves small sprites at their own size (Diamond is 160 px), so a thumbnail URL means it scaled down a bigger render or screenshot, and those are drawn smoothly. A `/thumb/` `.art` also gets a radial mask, which softens a screenshot's rectangular edges.

## Sounds

Every sound but one is synthesized with Web Audio when it plays (`SOUNDS`, played through `play()`). The exception is the items' explosion (`explode`, see [items.md](items.md)): one of Minecraft's own four explosion sounds, fetched from the wiki and pitched down at random as the game does, with a synthesized rumble until they are in. The rest are original, in the spirit of note blocks and the XP orb, not copies of Minecraft's: a link click is a pling that climbs a pentatonic scale with each click of the game (played when the navigation starts, so a slow load doesn't delay it), Back falls two notes, a dead link thuds (the frame posts `{mwr:'dead'}`), the checkpoint chimes from `celebrateCheckpoint`, and `finish` plays a fanfare for a win, a longer one for a new personal best, a sad trombone for Six clicks and two falling notes for giving up. The `AudioContext` is made on the first sound, which always follows a click or key press, as browsers require, and any audio error is swallowed. Sound is on by default, muted with the Sound checkbox beside Theme or the speaker button in the game header (`setSound`). With Distance on, a click that got closer also dings (`closer`), a step higher the fewer clicks are left: see Effects.

## Effects

Sparks are drawn over everything on `#fx`, a canvas the size of the window above the result window that takes no clicks, hidden while nothing is drawn; `effect(name, from)` plays one of `EFFECTS` from a point on the page, and `fxFrame` draws them, a square each, with a frame only while there are any. A link click (`navigate`'s `from`, from the frame's click point, or Back's button) sends seven sparks curving into the click count, which bumps (`.bump`) as the first lands; a navigation with no click, as Retry, just bumps it. A dead link puffs grey where it was clicked, a checkpoint or find bursts from its banner (`celebrateCheckpoint`), a win bursts from the result's heading in the topic colours, and a new personal best twice, bigger and in gold (`--viz-items`). A loss and giving up have none. None of these says how a game is going. Distance's cheer (`cheer`, from `frameReady`) does: once the article shows and both counts are measured, a click that got closer (`gotCloser`: as `noCloser` asks, to the same objective, so never one reaching a checkpoint or a find, and never under Exact clicks) dings, sparkles from the count and lights it (`.hint.closer`) until the next click. A click that didn't gets nothing, no colder sound or colour: Hot/cold's warnings were dropped for feeling like being told off, and the result's path is where those clicks are marked. Reduce motion draws no sparks and stops the bumps; the sounds still play. Every effect swallows its errors like `play()`, since they run inside `navigate` and `finish`.

## Timer

The Timer checkbox beside Sound (`#timerToggle`, remembered as `mwr.timer`) is for relaxed play. It is on by default, since the game is a race: clicks and time are its score, and the daily is meant for comparing results. `startGame` copies it into `game.timed`, so a game is timed or not throughout, and there is no switch in the game header. An untimed game hides the clock (`#hudTimeStat`, and no interval updating it), says nothing about time in the Give-up dialog or the peek, and its result has only the Clicks and Par tiles (`.tiles.untimed`). The clock still runs underneath, which keeps `navigate` and the pauses as they are, but `finish` records no `ms`, so the result, its personal-best note, Share and the daily box say clicks alone (`wonText`), and Recent games shows a dash for its time.

## Reduce motion

The Reduce motion checkbox beside Timer (`#motionToggle`, remembered as `mwr.reduceMotion`, off by default) stops everything that moves by itself. It is on in effect while the system asks for reduced motion (`SYSTEM_CALM`), which ticks the box and holds it, with a tooltip saying why; `calm()` is either. `setCalm` saves it and `syncCalm` sets the box and the `calm` class on `:root`, which turns off the banners', the checkpoint flash's and the bumps' animations and slows the busy dots. While it is on there are no sparks (see Effects) and the background draws still frames only, drawn on a change: no drift, pulses, rolling or cursor, the wave already passed, and the facts line doesn't change by itself. Ticking it, or the system's setting changing, marks the cards again (`bgPair`). It is the one change on the setup screen outside the Background box that leaves Roll by itself on. The wiki's own animations in the article, such as crafting grids cycling, are the article's and stay.

## Background

Behind the setup screen is a picture of the wiki on a fixed canvas (`#bgCanvas`), picked in the Background box at the top right (`#bgOpts`, a `<details>` beside Theme; on a phone it opens in the page). All of it is worked out in the page from `wiki-data.js` and the link graph, which the setup screen loads for par anyway: no generated file holds any of it.

- *Constellation* (`buildSky`): the 64 best-linked pool articles of each topic as stars, sized by how many articles link to them, each joined by a line to the three it links with (either way) that share the most links with it. Each topic is laid out on its own (Fruchterman-Reingold, seeded, so the sky is the same on every visit) into a disc with a faint nebula, the five discs around the screen (`SKY_DISCS`), so lines between topics cross it. It is made in an idle moment the first time it shows once the graph is in (not for the map or Off), and turns a quarter on an upright screen. The best-linked stars are named where the names don't overlap, and pulses (`skyPulse`) play games of their own along the links, from a star to one at least three clicks away, naming the star they start from and, once there, the one they reach. A star shows one name at a time, always above it: a start or target marker's first, then the cursor's, then a pulse's, then its plain label, and the plain labels also keep clear of the markers' and pulses' names.
- *Redstone map* (`buildGrid`): every article but the subpages and spin-off pages (`MAP_PAGES`) as one block, laid along a Gilbert curve (a Hilbert curve for any rectangle) sized to the screen, in patches of about 40 of one group (each topic, the Content families together, the rest), with every group's patches spread along the curve, so it reads as a world map's biomes. A block is brighter the more articles link to it.
- *Auto*, the default, is the constellation, and the redstone map at a phone's width (720 px and below, `BG_PHONE`), where the clusters are cramped. *Off* hides the canvas and the facts line.

What it marks (`bgPair`, called by `setupPar` whenever par is worked out again, so on any change of the cards, rules, difficulty or Content): the cards in use, each labelled with its card's tag (`cardBox`), and nothing past the start under Blind target. Its settings, as checkboxes in the box:

- *Wave from the start* (off by default): every page lights at its clicks from the start (`distancesFrom`, so as the target par counts them, under the setup's difficulty and Content, with the cards marked kept as par keeps them, so a hub card on Hard is reached), spread a little within each click so it ripples (`makeWave`): a flash as it arrives, then a glow that weakens with each click, like a redstone signal (`waveLight`). A stop after the start shows faint until the wave reaches it.
- *Show the route (spoiler)* (off by default): the setup's par route (`setup.route`, kept by `setupPar`, and none under Blind target), dashed.
- *Roll by itself* (on by default): the background marks pool pairs of its own (`bgRoll`), at least two clicks apart on the setup's difficulty, with their wave and route as the settings say, a new one once the wave has settled. Any change on the setup screen outside the Background box, Reduce motion and the items' button (a click on a button, link or control, a change, typing) turns it off (`bgSetting`), saved, so it stays off until it is ticked again; the background then follows the cards.

The cursor, with a mouse or pen (by the pointer's own type, so a mouse on a touch screen counts too; a touch is ignored) and motion not reduced (`bgFollows`): on the constellation the stars within 120 px make way, the more the nearer (`bgLens`), and the nearest within 60 px brightens and shows its name (its plain label or a pulse's name giving way, and a marked star keeping its marker's label instead), with its lines lit in its topic's colour (`bgNamed`); on the redstone map a torch lights the blocks around it, light 8 on its block and a level less each block away, counted along the rows and columns as the game counts light (`TORCH_DARK`, `TORCH_LIGHT`). It follows the pointer anywhere on the page (the canvas lets clicks through, so the document tracks it), which only shows where the background does, and goes when the pointer leaves the window. `bg.glow` eases it in and out, and each star eases its own push and name (`bgEase`), so nothing jumps.

The facts line (`#bgFacts`, above the footer, `bgNextFact`) changes every 7 seconds, and waits while it is hidden (in play, or Off). The wiki's facts (`bgFacts`): the snapshot's pages and date, the most linked page, each topic's size and best-linked article, and once the graph is in, the links between the pages, the page linking to the most and how many have no link to them outside a navbox; and how far apart pool articles are on the setup's difficulty (`bgSample`: from 40 seeded starts to every pool article, a start in each idle moment, kept per difficulty, and not worked out while the background is Off), the average, the share 2 clicks or fewer apart and the farthest pair. Once the cards are known their facts take turns with those (`bgPairFacts`): the start's links each way and how many pages are 1 and 2 clicks from it, and the target's links, never under Blind target.

*Background only* (`bgView`): the round button fixed in the setup screen's bottom right corner (`#btnBgView`, ✦, hidden with Off) hides everything on the setup screen but the Background box, open and fixed at the top right, the facts line, fixed along the bottom, and itself, now ✕ (class `bg-view` on `<body>`: `visibility`, unlike `display`, lets those three show inside the hidden screen, and the page doesn't scroll). The picture is 1.6 times as strong there, up to full strength (`bgTheme`), with nothing to read in front of it. The button again, or Escape, brings the screen back, and so does picking Off, or a game starting (`bgShow`). The button sits under a modal (`z-index` 5). Neither the button nor the box counts as a change for Roll by itself, so the background keeps showing pairs of its own.

It draws about 30 frames a second (`bgFrame`) while the setup screen shows and the page is in view: `startGame` and `toSetup` call `bgShow`. Text straight on the background on the setup screen has a halo of `--bg`, so the picture never cuts into it.

## The game header

The game header (`renderObjective`, redrawn when the objective changes) has the game and its extra rules as labels (`#hudTags`) with how far along it is ("1 of 2 checkpoints reached", "Found 1 of 3", "On the way back", Exact clicks's count), and with Distance on the latest arrival's count beside them (`#hudHint`, from `renderHud`). Under them is the route (`#hudRoute`): every stop as a chip, done (✓, a button), now (the objective, holding `#hudImg`, `#hudLbl` and `#hudGoal`) or ahead (dashed), joined by arrows; for Scavenger the things to find, found ones ticked with what found them. A route of more than `ROUTE_FULL` (3) stops shrinks the done chips to their ✓ (their names stay in a tooltip), and a click on one opens them all (`.route.open`); the row scrolls sideways when it still doesn't fit, kept on the objective by `scrollRouteToNow` (also on resize). The ? button beside it is the peek. On the right are the clicks (with a limit, "of 6" or "of exactly 4" and a dot a click), par (`#hudParStat`; see [par.md](par.md)), time (unless the Timer is off), sound, Back and Give up.

## Phones

At 720 px and below the setup cards stack, the options at the top wrap, the Background box opens in the page, Auto's background is the redstone map, Start game spans the width, and Recent games shows each game as a card (the labels and the day, the route, the result and time) instead of table columns. On a phone held upright (also taller than 500 px) the game header puts the labels and the route on their own rows above clicks, time and the buttons, Back shows only its arrow, and the path sidebar becomes one line of breadcrumbs that scrolls sideways, kept at its newest entry by `scrollPathToEnd` (also on resize, as turning the phone switches layouts), with a faded left edge while there is more to the left (`markPathScroll`), Hide beside it and the credit under it. Any screen 500 px tall or less, such as a phone on its side, keeps the sidebar at 160 px instead. On touch screens (`pointer: coarse`) inputs use 16 px text so iOS doesn't zoom on focus and the small controls are bigger. The game is `100dvh` tall so phone toolbars don't cover it. Inside the frame the article's padding narrows below 600 px, and the hover tint only applies with `(hover: hover)`, since on a touch screen it would stay on the tapped link. The wiki's own CSS already makes wide tables scroll sideways.
